Definition & headline finding
Consecutive means neighbors on the number line (|a−b|=1), regardless of draw order. Example: 23, 3, 34, 17, 2, 39 → sorted 2, 3, … → 2 and 3 count. About 55–58% of draws have at least one such pair — higher than intuition suggests, because most 6/42 combinations leave a gap of 1 somewhere. Jackpot-winning draws are not enriched for this (54.9% vs 58.8% with no winner).

1. Why consecutive feels rare but isn’t

Pick 6 distinct numbers from 1–42. After sorting, ask: does any adjacent pair differ by exactly 1?

Exact math

Total combos C(42,6) = 5,245,786. Combos with no consecutive numbers = C(37,6) = 2,324,784 (map gaps). So 55.68% of all tickets already contain ≥1 consecutive pair — before looking at any real draw.

Intuition trap: there are many more non-adjacent number pairs in the pool, but a 6-number ticket creates 5 sorted gaps. It is hard for all five gaps to stay ≥2 across 1…42.

Pair-count distribution (obs vs theory)

Definition: count of sorted adjacent gaps equal to 1 (a triple like 7-8-9 = 2 pairs). z ≈ 1.6 vs theory for “≥1 pair” — not significant (p ≈ 0.11).

Finding: sequential pairs ≠ jackpot predictor
Roughly 55% of jackpot-winning draws contain a consecutive pair — but ~56% of all fair 6/42 draws do. Winning draws are not special on this metric; no-winner draws show consecutive pairs slightly more often.

Practical takeaway: picking consecutive numbers does not improve or hurt jackpot odds of the machine. It may affect how many people share a jackpot if consecutive picks are popular — this dataset only has winner counts, not ticket compositions.

3. Stronger insight: birthday-range bias

This is the real pattern
When all 6 drawn numbers are ≤31 (calendar/birthday range), the draw produced a jackpot winner 40.8% of the time — vs 14.5% overall. Draws with several numbers ≥32 win far less often. Machine frequencies are still uniform; players cluster on 1–31.

6. Have any winning combos won again?

Finding: have exact winning combinations repeated?
Across 919 unique draw dates: zero exact 6-number combinations repeated on a later date. The two “duplicates” in the sheet are same-day copy-paste rows (2021-10-14 and 2022-09-17), not real re-draws.
CheckResult
Same combo on different dates0
Same-day sheet dupes (dropped)2
Unique combos in sample919
Total possible 6/42 tickets5,245,786
Expected true repeats in 919 draws~0.08
P(≥1 true repeat by chance)~7.7%

So “wala pang ulit” is normal — not proof that past winners can’t come back. With this sample size you’d usually see zero repeats.

Avoiding past winners is almost useless
Skipping all 919 past combos removes only 919 / 5,245,786 ≈ 0.0175% of the ticket space. Each new draw is independent: a past winner still has probability 1/5,245,786 — same as any other unused combo. They are not “instant losses.”

Excluding historical winners feels like a large filter, but it barely shrinks the pool. Jackpot odds stay ~1 in 5.25 million either way.

Bottom line

Consecutive pairs Settled
Expected ~56% of the time. Observed 58%. Common as a base rate — not a winner filter.
Birthday bias Actionable
All-≤31 draws hit jackpot ~41% of the time. Want less sharing? Lean into 32–42 more than the crowd does.
Anomalies Fair draw
No durable machine bias. Quarterly consec spikes / droughts look like normal variance. Clean 2 sheet dupes.
Past winners Strategy myth
No combo repeated across dates — expected. Avoiding them removes ~0.02% of tickets; they are not instant losses.
